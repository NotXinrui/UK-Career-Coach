# Keyword Library

Starting banks of terms by role track. They are **seeds, not a verified list**: confirm each term against the actual JDs you are targeting before relying on it, and add only terms you can truthfully support.

## Track A: Project / programme management and PMO
Typical settings: corporates, consultancies, public bodies, delivery teams.

Terms: project lifecycle, programme governance, portfolio management, PMO, RAG status, RAID log, risk register, milestone tracking, dependency management, change control, benefits realisation, resource and capacity planning, executive reporting, stakeholder management, lessons learned, assurance, Gantt, budget forecasting, agile delivery, scrum, Jira, MS Project, Primavera P6, Power BI.

Coaching note: use named tools only if genuinely used. Governance vocabulary (RAG, RAID) often describes existing practice and can be named without new claims.

## Track B: Digital transformation and AI
Typical settings: technology firms, digital consultancies, in-house transformation teams.

Terms: digital transformation, technology programme, change management, process automation, workflow digitisation, data governance, AI governance, responsible AI, AI roadmap, use-case prioritisation, intake and prioritisation, cross-functional delivery, product lifecycle, adoption, stakeholder alignment, agile, sprint planning, feasibility assessment.

Coaching note: position AI knowledge as the ability to govern and deliver AI work, unless the user's evidence shows hands-on engineering.

## Track C: Business analysis and consulting
Typical settings: consultancies, advisory teams, in-house analysis functions.

Terms: requirements gathering, business case, process mapping, gap analysis, stakeholder interviews, workshop facilitation, user stories, acceptance criteria, impact assessment, operating model, benefits case, options appraisal, data analysis, KPIs and OKRs, recommendations and insight, client-ready deliverables.

Coaching note: consulting JDs often reward structured problem framing and evidence of influence without authority; check the CV shows both.

## Adding your own track
Add a section per role family you target: typical settings, 15–25 terms taken from real JDs, and a one-line coaching note on common gaps.

## ATS optimisation rules

1. Mirror the JD's wording where the user genuinely has the experience; do not paraphrase a specific term away.
2. Place the highest-priority terms in the first three bullets of each relevant role.
3. In a skills section, list only tools and methods actually used.
4. Avoid keyword stuffing. Modern systems and human readers both look for context.
5. Submit in the format the employer requests; if none is stated, check the application page, since requirements differ.
